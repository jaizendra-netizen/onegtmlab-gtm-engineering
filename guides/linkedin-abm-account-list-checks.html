Seven checks before you upload a LinkedIn ABM account list

LinkedIn account-based marketing means you upload a list of the companies you want to win, show ads only to the buyers inside those companies, and follow up with the accounts that engage. The ad is rarely what decides whether it works. The list is. These are seven checks we run on a list before a single ad goes live.

1. Start from companies that already know you

The best list begins with companies already reacting to your founder and your brand. When people at target companies like, comment and view, the ad becomes the second or third touch, not the first. Add past wins, companies showing buying signals such as hiring for the role you sell to, and companies engaging with competitors' content.

2. Qualify by reading what each company does

Do not filter on LinkedIn's industry field. Each company picked its industry tag years ago from a short fixed list, so one bucket can hold vendors, consultancies, managed service providers and in-house teams alike. Instead, have an AI model read each company's own description, its LinkedIn About text, and its homepage when the About text is thin. Ask for a plain-label verdict, a reason and a confidence level for every company, so anyone on the team can audit the call.

3. Build exclusions as their own list

Competitors, customers, open deals, sales-tool vendors and placeholder companies belong in an uploaded exclusion list, not an afterthought. In Campaign Manager an exclusion only overrides an inclusion on the same Company Page, so check overlaps by Page ID before uploading. A company that exists under two different Pages can slip through.

4. Upload with the LinkedIn Page URL

Campaign Manager's company list upload accepts company name, website, email domain, LinkedIn Page URL or stock symbol, and its own dialog says that for the best match results you should include the LinkedIn page URL. Lists with Page URLs match far better than lists with names only.

5. Size the audience before you spend

The forecast in Campaign Manager is free. Data providers' company counts are useful for sizing outbound, but they say nothing about how many LinkedIn members you will reach. Size ads only with LinkedIn's own forecast. A list that is too small does not serve, and one that is too broad spends on the wrong people.

6. Layer the buyer titles on top

The targeting rule is the company list AND the buyer job titles, minus the exclusion list. That way only the buyers inside the accounts see the ad.

7. Measure by company, not by click

Campaign Manager's Companies report shows engagement for every account on your list. Judge the programme by which target companies engaged, because that is what your sales team can act on.

Why most programmes underperform

The problems are almost always upstream of the ad. The list is built from industry tags. Nobody excludes anything, so competitors and existing customers eat the budget. The list is cold, which means months of impressions before anyone engages. And nobody checks the size before spending. Each of the seven checks above fixes one of these.

One lesson worth the money

Past a certain point, list size stops being the lever. Small agencies, for instance, have only a handful of people each on LinkedIn, so adding hundreds more of them barely moves the audience. Budget and creative matter more than another few hundred companies.
